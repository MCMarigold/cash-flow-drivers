October 2026 checkpoint · Drivers 1, 2, 4, 5 & 6 cover full October (Oct 1–31) · Driver 9 averages three full Mon–Sun weeks (Oct 5–25) · Drivers 3, 7 & 8 reflect live state as of Nov 2–3 · updated Nov 3, ahead of the Nov 5 L10
Big Picture

This Month at a Glance

Most October numbers cover the full calendar month, and several drivers changed definition or goals, so some comparisons with September are approximate. Five moved toward target. Inspector/Tech Efficiency for inspectors rose from 74.7% to 77.0%. Cross-Selling went from 54.0% to 59.6%, now 0.4 points under its raised 60% target. Wrong People / Wrong Seats went from 83% to 88% after the leadership seat updates. AI Process Efficiencies went from 6.4 to 6.6 hrs/wk with 19 live processes (the 15-process goal is met), and Average Employee Tenure rose from 2.56 to 2.77 years, above its 2.2 guardrail. Two moved away. Inspector Available Capacity went from 10.4% to 18.2%, about 8 points over the top of its 5–10% range. MHS Marketing Leads fell from 13.0 to 8.1/wk, partly because RHM quotes no longer count. Follow-Up Close Rate (now Mitigation only, 41.4%) and Sales KPIs Achieved (41.7%, against new goals) are not directly comparable to September.

Three items look most worth time at the Nov 5 L10, in this order. First, Inspector Available Capacity has flipped from a shortage question to a surplus one. About 1.7 inspectors' worth of slots were open, and leads fell in the same month, so it reads best next to Driver 2. Second, Follow-Up Close Rate has 33 of 70 quotes still undecided, 22 of them sent in the last two weeks, so the rate will move and a follow-up pass on those quotes could have the biggest effect. Third, Sales KPIs Achieved carries a Decide item: Sales Calls missed its 125/wk goal in every October week, so it is worth deciding whether the goal still fits the team's capacity.

Closest to target: Cross-Selling is 2 bookings from 60%, Wrong People / Wrong Seats is 2 points from 90%, and Tenure is holding above its guardrail. A few housekeeping items are worth a quick check before the meeting. Two of the five flagged seats on Driver 3 are for people who have since left, and one person who has left is still listed as active on Driver 8. Driver 5's Service Tech group is only two people this month. And Driver 2's and Driver 4's October definitions differ from September's, so say so when presenting them.

Available capacity went from 10.4% in September to 18.2% for full October, about 8 points over the 10% ceiling. The team had 526.5 available slots and used 430.9 weighted slots, and all 14 people on the team finished the month above 10% available. By Mon–Sun week it ran 12.6% (Oct 5–11), 14.5%, 10.8%, then 29.5% for Oct 26–Nov 1, when bookings fell to 92. The slow-season slot rules also started on Oct 5. The team had about 73 more slots than it needed to sit at the 5% floor, roughly 1.7 standard inspectors. The ~$364K–$416K estimate on the methodology page is built around adding capacity to reach that floor, so this month points the other way: there is room, not a shortage.

Ideas To Close The Gap
  • Read this next to Driver 2. Leads fell from 13.0/wk to 8.1/wk in the same month capacity opened up, so part of the picture may be demand rather than staffing.
  • With about 1.7 inspectors' worth of open slots, a hiring decision doesn't look pressing on October's numbers. The November read will show whether the last week's drop was seasonal or a new level.
  • If open slots are a one-month pattern, a short-notice push to agents or past clients is a way to fill them without changing the schedule.
  • Joel Terry is excluded from this page and Travis East is counted as a trainee. If either of those rules changes, re-run the month before comparing it with September.

Driver 6 · MHI
Cross-Selling / Ancillary Attach Rate
Owner: Maggie
59.6%
Target: 60%
What Happened

The attach rate was 59.6% for full October, up from 54.0% on the Sep 1–20 page and 57.7% in August, and 0.4 points under the new 60% target (raised from 55%). That is 238 of 399 in-scope inspections with at least one add-on, 2 short of the 240 needed. Add-on revenue was $84,772, or $212 per inspection (up from $191). Radon (43.1%) and sewer scope (37.8%) carry the number, as in every period, and 161 inspections (40.4%) had no add-on at all. The ~$72.8K estimate was sized for the older goal, so it may need updating now that the target is 60%. The September column is a Sep 1–20 page, not a full-month re-cut, so per-inspection figures compare more fairly than totals.

Ideas To Close The Gap
  • The target is within two bookings, so small changes in how add-ons are offered on the day could decide whether the month clears it.
  • The 161 inspections with no add-on are the pool to look at: which services were offered, and whether radon and sewer were raised on every one.
  • Everything below radon and sewer attaches on under 5% of inspections. Chimney, mold and water testing carry higher fees ($275, $393 and $331 average), so small lifts there are worth more per booking.
  • Re-size the annual impact for the 60% goal so the methodology page and the target agree.

Goal: 40/wk
What Happened

October had 36 counted leads, or 8.1/wk over 4.43 weeks, down from 13.0/wk in September and 31.9/wk short of the 40/wk goal. Part of the drop is the definition: RHM first-time quotes (2.0/wk in September) are no longer counted, and email is no longer a separate source because those leads now land in HCP. The three full Mon–Sun weeks came in at 8, 10 and 9, right at the 9/wk baseline. Radon-test-first leads rose to 1.8/wk (1.3 in September) while direct-to-estimate leads eased from 7.3 to 6.3/wk. Another 13 contacts were MHI referrals and are not counted. Of the 36 counted leads, 18 have an install booked, in progress or completed (6 installed), and late-October leads have had little time to move. The ~$409K estimate still depends on how well Driver 4 converts them.

Ideas To Close The Gap
  • The jump to 40/wk is large next to a 9/wk baseline. It is worth deciding what mix of sources would plausibly get there, and which of them are currently counted.
  • Five counted leads have nothing booked yet and ten have an estimate with no install. A quick follow-up pass on those would help both this driver and Driver 4.
  • Read this next to Driver 1. Open inspector slots and fewer leads in the same month suggest looking at demand, not just capacity.
  • Check that the October counting rules (RHM excluded, email folded into HCP) are what the team wants going forward, so November compares cleanly.

What Happened

The Mitigation close rate for October is 41.4% (29 accepted of 70 quotes sent), 28.6 points under the 70% target. The rate is held down mostly by the undecided pile. 33 of 70 quotes are still open and 22 of those were sent on Oct 19 or later, while 29 of the 37 quotes already decided were accepted (78%). Accepted quotes total $57,310; closing the sent pipeline at 70% would have meant about $42,548 more. 11 of the 29 accepted jobs are completed and billed. The annual impact figure is still open between ~$100K on the methodology page and a higher monthly extrapolation.

Ideas To Close The Gap
  • The 22 quotes sent in the last two weeks are the ones most likely to move the rate, so a follow-up pass on them will show the biggest effect.
  • 18 records with a $0 price (visits not yet priced) are left out of the count. A clear rule for when a quote counts as sent would keep the rate steady month to month.
  • Compare the weekly rates: quotes created Oct 1–18 closed at about half or better, so the later weeks may recover as they age.
  • Settle which annual impact figure the dashboard carries so the target and the dollar value agree.

What Happened

Hours saved are 6.6 a week, up slightly from 6.4 in September, and live processes went from 17 to 19, so the 15-process goal is met while the 40 hrs/wk goal is 33.4 hours away (about 16% of the way). At the current pace the annual labor value is $10,296 against $62,400 at goal, and 103 one-time hours are credited separately. Of the 6.6 hours, 3.4 are measured, 2.0 estimated and 1.2 rough. The biggest single gain is the Service Technician Points Counter at 1.6 hrs/wk, which is built but not counted because it is not yet scheduled. Even if every proposed process went live, the total would reach about 8.4 hrs/wk.

Ideas To Close The Gap
  • Scheduling the Points Counter is the largest single step, and it would move the number more than any other item in the log.
  • Closing a 33-hour gap likely means a few larger workflows rather than many small ones. Most live processes save under an hour a week each.
  • Moving Rough estimates to Measured would make the number more defensible, which is an open question on the page.
  • Decide whether a flat $30/hr is still the right rate for the dollar value, or whether each process should use the rate of whoever's time was freed.
