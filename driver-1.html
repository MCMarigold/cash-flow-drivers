Methodology

  • Numerator (weighted): each booking's weight = its Spectora duration ÷ the team's average duration (3.26 hrs = "1 slot"). TISH/Brett Fredrichs excluded. Multi-inspector bookings count full duration for both people.
  • Denominator (slots): standard day = 2 slots, extended "long" day (8:30–7:00) = 3 slots.
  • Long-day assignments: Aaron Vandeventer (Mon), Mitchell Bowser/Matt Clarity/Joel Terry (Tue), Kelly Brill (Thu), Stephen Hines (Mon+Thu, alternating weeks).
  • Saturday rotation: +2 slots/month unless starred (flex, no bonus). Starred: Matt Mossey, Travis East, Mitchell Bowser, Joel Terry, Anthony Link-Danko, Kyle Kanipes. Matt Clarity gets +4 — confirmed he works both his regular Week 4 Saturday and the August 5th-Saturday.
  • PTO: docks the actual day's slot value (2 or 3), from the calendar export plus every confirmed block from the manual open-slot review.
  • Mark Rahja: 1 slot/day, only when needed — excluded from core team. Dan Kosel: 9 slots/week (no Monday morning) — included.
  • New hires (Joel Terry, Travis East): counted only from first solo inspection forward.
  • Open-slot review: every gap gets checked via a standalone interactive checklist artifact before this sheet is finalized — see Step 5 of the skill.

Hiring Impact (from Blake's meeting notes)

Original note: "Unknown until we keep adding inspectors and this goes down. Each additional inspector brings approximately $7,008 a week." That figure checks out against the original checklist's $364–416K driver impact: $7,008 × 52 weeks = $364,416 — the dollar range was never "5% of revenue," it's the value of hiring enough inspectors to close the gap, priced per hire.

Using August's real numbers to answer "how many": holding demand (weighted consumption, 510.4) constant, the team needs 537.3 total slots to reach 5% available — a gap of 47.3 slots from the current 490. A standard inspector (no long day, no Saturday) contributes roughly 42 slots/month in this model, so closing the gap takes about 1.1 additional standard hires — essentially one person, worth roughly the $364K figure already in the checklist. This assumes demand holds steady; a hire that also grows total bookings would need more slots to reach the same %, and one who takes on a long-day or Saturday rotation would close the gap faster than this estimate.
