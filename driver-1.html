October 1–31, 2026 — Inspector Available Capacity

Weighted slots consumed ÷ available slots, whole inspector team. Joel Terry excluded.

Inspector available capacity measures how much inspection capacity is actually available after accounting for scheduled work. The goal is to keep available capacity between 5% and 10%. At least 5% gives the team enough room to absorb demand without consistently operating over capacity, and more than 10% open slots is not sustainable revenue-wise.

The estimated annual financial impact is approximately $364K–$416K. This represents a potential annual revenue opportunity associated with adding enough inspector capacity to reach the 5% floor of the target range. The underlying model estimates about $7,008 in additional weekly revenue per additional inspector, annualized to $364,416. This is a revenue opportunity, not direct cash savings, and the total increases if more than one inspector is needed.

Methodology

  • Numerator (weighted): each booking's weight = its Spectora duration ÷ 3.25 hrs (the locked "1 slot"). The weekly log checked each week's actual average against that value and it never drifted more than 0.25 hrs, so the locked value is used for the whole month (September's page used that month's own average, 3.26 hrs, which is effectively the same). TISH/Brett Fredrichs, Blake Pederson-Perovich commercial jobs and the unassigned "Marigold Home Inspections" placeholder rows are excluded. Multi-inspector bookings count full duration for each person; Maggie ruled the 3-person jobs in the week of Oct 19 are big properties, not padding.
  • Window: Oct 1–31, 2026 — 22 workdays (no company holiday) and five Saturdays (Oct 3, 10, 17, 24, 31). Daily rows come from the Inspector Day Capacity table in Airtable, every row marked "Actual (past)".
  • Slot rules by date: Oct 1–4 use the earlier rules (2 slots a day, 3-slot long days, Dan Kosel 9 a week, +2 for an unstarred Saturday). From Mon Oct 5 the slow-season rules apply: no 3-slot days, Dan Kosel 1 slot a day, everyone who works a Saturday gets +2 slots and has that week's Thursday flexed to 0, starred designates included. A flexed Thursday is never docked as time off.
  • Saturdays: Oct 3 Stephen Hines (swap for Matt Mossey) and Travis East; Oct 10 Aaron Vandeventer (Mitchell Bowser worked the Sunday shift instead); Oct 17 Kelly Brill and Kyle Kanipes (swaps logged); Oct 24 Matt Clarity, Matt Mossey and Walker Russek (swaps logged); Oct 31 Travis East (5th Saturday) plus Aaron Vandeventer at 1 slot with Thu 10/29 flexed (Maggie, Nov 3).
  • PTO and blocks: docks the actual day's slot value, half for AM/PM entries, from the PTO calendar plus every "Off"/"Out"/block entry in the Spectora events export, with Maggie's weekly rulings applied (block/BPP/off = off). Blocks under 3 hrs are not docked unless Maggie said so (Kyle Grovenstein's Wed 10/21 11:30–3:30 hold was docked as a half day).
  • Drive-time rule (Oct 26): 3.25+ hrs of Drive blocks on one day docks 1 slot that day, once per person per day. Applied only to the week of Oct 19; no other week had a day that long.
  • Team (Nov 3): everyone with slots except Joel Terry (extended leave, sample too small), per Maggie. Dan Kosel is included at his scheduled slots (his day-by-day availability changes are reflected in his docks). Trainee Travis East is included (Oct 12 ruling). These exclusions apply to October; ask again next run.
  • Data sources: Spectora inspections and events, the live 2026-27 Inspector/Tech Schedule sheet, the PTO calendar and Airtable were pulled fresh for each weekly log run (Oct 7, 11, 19, 26 and Nov 2–3). October per-inspector totals were summed from the Airtable daily rows on Nov 3 and reconcile to the weekly log's final rows.
  • Target range (Oct 7, 2026): 5–10% available. Inside the range is on target; below 5% is too tight and above 10% is too loose (not sustainable revenue-wise). Outside the range shows in the off-target color either way.

Hiring impact

Per Curtis's original meeting notes: "Each additional inspector brings approximately $7,008 a week." This is the revenue base behind the checklist's $364–416K driver impact — $7,008 × 52 weeks = $364,416 for one hire. Confirmed: the dollar figure was never "5% of a separate MHI/MHS revenue pool," it's the direct value of hiring enough inspectors to close the availability gap.

Holding demand (weighted consumption, 430.9) constant, the team needs 453.6 total slots to sit at 5% available. That's 72.9 fewer than the 526.5 it had this month. A standard inspector contributes about 44 slots over these 22 workdays, so October shows no additional hire needed, with about 1.7 of a standard inspector's worth of room above the 5% line. That margin is a slow-season effect, not a structural surplus: the last week of October alone ran at 29.5% available, and the earlier rules (3-slot long days) only applied to Oct 1–4. Assumes demand holds steady; a hire who also grows total bookings needs more slots to reach the same %.

Target band in slots: with the 5–10% target, the same demand (430.9) is on target with anywhere from 453.6 to 478.8 total slots. The team had 526.5, about 48 slots (roughly 1.1 of a standard inspector) above the 10% ceiling, so October is too loose rather than short of capacity.
