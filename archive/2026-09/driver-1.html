The estimated annual financial impact is approximately $364K–$416K. This represents a potential annual revenue opportunity associated with adding enough inspector capacity to reach the 5% availability target. The underlying model estimates about $7,008 in additional weekly revenue per additional inspector, annualized to $364,416. This is a revenue opportunity, not direct cash savings, and the total increases if more than one inspector is needed.

Methodology

  • Numerator (weighted): each booking's weight = its Spectora duration ÷ the team's average duration (3.21 hrs = "1 slot" this window, vs. 3.26 in August). TISH/Brett Fredrichs excluded (10 TISH bookings — technician, not inspector). Blake Pederson-Perovich (2 bookings) and the unassigned "Marigold Home Inspections" placeholder (15 ancillary-only bookings) excluded. Multi-inspector bookings count full duration for both people.
  • Sanity check on that 3.21-hr slot: a normal 2-slot day works out to 6.42 hrs actually inspecting. On an 8-hour workday, that leaves about 1.6 hrs for lunch and drive time. A long day's 3 slots come to 9.64 hrs inspecting within the 10.5-hour (8:30–7:00) window, leaving about 52 minutes for lunch and drive combined — still tight, and part of why long days lean on smaller jobs (condos/townhomes) to fit a 3rd inspection in at all.
  • Window: Sep 1–20, 2026 — 13 workdays (Labor Day, Sep 7, closed and not counted) and three Saturdays (Sep 5, 12, 19). Week-4 Saturday designates (Kyle Grovenstein, Kyle Kanipes, Matt Clarity) have no regular Saturday inside the window.
  • Denominator (slots): standard day = 2 slots, extended "long" day (8:30–7:00) = 3 slots.
  • Long-day assignments: Aaron Vandeventer (Mon), Mitchell Bowser/Matt Clarity/Joel Terry (Tue), Kelly Brill (Thu), Stephen Hines (Mon+Thu, alternating weeks — his "Early Week" entries on Sep 3, 14 and 17 place his late week on Sep 7–11, so Thu Sep 10 is his only long day in the window). Late-night rows without the 8:30–7:00 note (Mossey/Grovenstein Wed, Kanipes/Link-Danko Thu, Travis East Mon) stay at 2 slots, same as August.
  • Saturday rotation: +2 slots per Saturday worked unless starred (flex, no bonus — their flex weekday isn't docked either). Starred: Mitchell Bowser, Joel Terry, Anthony Link-Danko, Kyle Kanipes. Logged swaps from the events export are applied to whoever actually held the shift: Matt Clarity (Sep 12, for Aaron), Kelly Brill (Sep 12), Tony Weinand (Sep 19, for Kelly), Walker Russek (Sep 5, half day = 1 slot). Matt Mossey's Sep 5 Saturday was logged "Off" and docked. Anthony Link-Danko flexed Sep 11 for his Sep 19 Saturday (a different week than usual), so that day isn't docked.
  • PTO: docks the actual day's slot value (2 or 3), half for AM/PM entries, from the calendar plus every "Off"/"Out" block in the Spectora events export. Kelly Brill's Drill Weekend (Sep 17–18) is docked from the events export — it isn't on the PTO calendar.
  • Mark Rahja: 1 slot/day, only when needed — excluded from core team. Dan Kosel: 9 slots/week (no Monday morning) — included. Aaron Vandeventer: excluded this run (light duty for most of the window).
  • New hires (Joel Terry, Travis East): solo-started Aug 14 (Employee Directory "First Day"). Excluded from the core team until each clears 100 lifetime inspections (Airtable Inspector Milestones) — currently at 44 and 51.
  • Open-slot review: every gap gets cross-checked against Spectora's own events export before falling back to manual confirmation. This month's export was the flat calendar-event CSV (not the categorized .xlsx), covering Sep 1–20 in full. Everything left after that went through Maggie's manual gap review (Sep 22): 1 block confirmed, 20 slots confirmed as real availability.

Hiring impact

Per Curtis's original meeting notes: "Each additional inspector brings approximately $7,008 a week." This is the revenue base behind the checklist's $364–416K driver impact — $7,008 × 52 weeks = $364,416 for one hire. Confirmed: the dollar figure was never "5% of a separate MHI/MHS revenue pool," it's the direct value of hiring enough inspectors to close the availability gap.

Holding demand (weighted consumption, 265.5) constant, the team needs 279.5 total slots to sit at 5% available. That's 4.5 fewer than the 284 it had in this window. A standard inspector (no long day, no Saturday) contributes about 26 slots over these 13 workdays, so this window shows no additional hire needed, with only about 0.2 of a standard inspector's worth of room above the 5% line. That's a thin margin on a partial month with heavy PTO and a soft Sep 16–18; August's full month needed about one hire (~$364K). Assumes demand holds steady; a hire who also grows total bookings needs more slots to reach the same %.
