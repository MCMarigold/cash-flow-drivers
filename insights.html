Big Picture

This Month at a Glance

This is the first month-over-month read on the dashboard, and most of September's numbers cover about 20 days, not a full month, so expect some movement at month-end. Four drivers moved toward target since August. Inspector Available Capacity swung from –3.7% to 6.5% and is now above its 5% target. Follow-Up Close Rate rose from 40.8% to 47.2% combined. AI Process Efficiencies went from 2 to 6.4 hrs/wk, and its 15-process count goal is now met with 17 live. MHS Marketing Leads now has a September build and rose from 11.5 to 13.0/wk. Three moved away. Inspector/Tech Efficiency fell in all three role groups. Cross-Selling slipped from 57.7% to 54.0%, 1 point under target. Wrong People / Wrong Seats dropped from 98% to 83%, below its 90% target for the first time, because 7 seats are now marked as failing. Sales KPIs and Tenure are unchanged.

Three items look most worth time at the Sept 24 L10, in this order. First, Inspector/Tech Efficiency is still the broadest slide on the board. Only 3 of 12 regular inspectors are at or above 80% (7 were in August), and Radon Tech dropped to 56.5%. Read it next to Driver 1: September's capacity cushion came from a short, PTO-heavy window with softer late bookings, so together they don't settle the hiring question yet. Second, Wrong People / Wrong Seats is the biggest new change this month. Some of the 7 flags are structural (a seat held by someone who also holds another seat, or two people sharing one seat), so it's worth separating seat-structure fixes from person-fit conversations. Third, Sales KPIs Achieved is still the widest gap at 18.75%, and it carries two Decide items meant for this L10: whether the 115/wk Sales Calls target still fits (last hit May 4), and whether the Estimates sent and won goals match current volume.

A few housekeeping items are worth a quick check before the meeting. Driver 9's Sep 17 Estimates sent and won are still blank on the Scorecard (page pulled Sept 22), so re-pull it if they get entered. Driver 3's page still says every seat defaults to a pass, even though 7 are now marked failing, so confirm those flags reflect real scoring. Driver 2 has an open Decide item that would move its headline from 13.0 to 11.7/wk.

What Happened

Capacity moved from –3.7% in August to 6.5% for Sep 1–20, just above the 5% target. That's 265.5 of 284 slots filled across the 12-person core team. Two things drove the swing. The window is only 13 workdays, with 48 core-team slots docked for PTO. Bookings also softened Sep 16–18, and the gap review confirmed those open slots were real availability, not unlogged time. The cushion is thin: about four more average-length inspections would have put the team below target. At this pace the page shows roughly 0.2 of a standard inspector's worth of room. August's full month modeled about one hire (~$364K/yr).

Ideas To Close The Gap
  • Treat September as one partial, PTO-heavy window rather than proof the capacity gap has closed. The full-month read will show whether 6.5% holds.
  • Keep the hiring conversation open until there are two or three normal-demand months to compare against August's –3.7%.
  • Aaron Vandeventer (light duty) was out of the core team this run. When he comes back, available capacity rises, but so does the denominator, so plan for both.

Driver 6 · MHI
Cross-Selling / Ancillary Attach Rate
Owner: Maggie
54.0%
Target: 55%
What Happened

The attach rate slipped from 57.7% in August to 54.0% for Sep 1–20. That's 1 point under target, or 3 attached bookings short of 55% on 289 in-scope inspections. It's a real change, not a scope effect: under August's exact rules, the rate would be 53.5%. The drop comes from single-family inspections (69.6% down to 62.2%, n=209), which had more jobs with no add-on at all. Radon and sewer attach rates are slightly up, and condo/townhome is roughly flat at 35.3%. Add-on revenue per inspection is $191, 2.5% below August but still above the Apr–Jun baseline.

Ideas To Close The Gap
  • Look at the single-family bookings with zero add-ons this period to see what they have in common (booking channel, agent, price point, or how quickly they were booked).
  • Chimney fell from 6.6% to 4.8%, which makes it the one add-on family that clearly lost ground and a narrow place to refocus.
  • Condo/townhome (35.3%) is still the largest structural gap. A tailored add-on offer for that segment remains the longer-term lever.

MHS Marketing Leads
Owner: Minh

Goal: 40/wk · Baseline: 9/wk
What Happened

The September build is in: 39 counted leads across three complete weeks (Aug 31 – Sep 20), or 13.0/wk. That's up from 11.5/wk in August, 44% above the 9/wk baseline, and 27/wk short of the 40/wk goal. Weekly counts were uneven. Sep 7–13 dipped to 8, while Sep 14–20 was the strongest week since tracking began at 18. The mix also shifted. Leads that start with a radon test fell to about a third of August's pace (1.3/wk vs. 3.75/wk), direct-to-estimate leads rose about 70% (7.3/wk vs. 4.25/wk), and RHM first-time quotes doubled to 2.0/wk. Another 14 contacts were MHI referrals, excluded from the count. One open Decide item matters for the headline: if an HCP "MHI" lead-source tag alone counts as a referral, the number drops to 11.7/wk. The ~$409K/yr potential still depends on how well Driver 4 converts these leads.

Ideas To Close The Gap
  • Settle the HCP "MHI" tag question before the L10 so the team is looking at one agreed headline, 13.0 or 11.7.
  • At least 2 counted web-form leads came in through a Google Ads click. Confirming whether Ads/LSA spend is tracked anywhere would show what paid channels are adding.
  • The drop in radon-test-first leads is the one source that clearly shrank. It's worth checking whether that reflects a change in how people book or a real dip in test demand.
  • Four of the six RHM quotes have no estimate on file yet. A quick follow-up keeps them from going stale while the RHM funnel is still young.
